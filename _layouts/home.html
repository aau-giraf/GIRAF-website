A system project at Aalborg University

Visual tools for autistic children with little or no verbal speech.

GIRAF is visual schedules, communication boards and meal plans, built by students and tested with a special-needs school.

See the apps Work on GIRAF

The project

GIRAF is a system project at Aalborg University's Department of Computer Science, developed with Egebakken Specialskole. The apps are designed and tested with the school in short iterations. They are prototypes, not yet a product in use.

The apps

Three apps.

All three are Flutter apps. Weekplanner runs on the shared giraf-core service for users, organisations and pictograms; VTA and Foodplanner still run their own backends.

On giraf-core

Weekplanner

Caregivers build a child's day and week from pictograms. The child sees what is happening and in what order, and ticks off each activity as it is done.

In active development

VTA: communication boards

Boards of visual artefacts, each with an image, a label and a sound, shared live between a child and a caregiver. Includes text-to-speech and video calling.

Foodplanner

Visual meal plans (madpakker) built from photos of real food, for children with selective eating. Based on the Picture Exchange Communication System.

How it's designed

Five principles.

From TEACCH and PECS.

  • 01

    Predictability

    What happens next is always visible.

  • 02

    Visual clarity

    One idea per card, shown in pictures first.

  • 03

    Simplicity

    As few steps as possible.

  • 04

    Consistency

    The same action looks and works the same way everywhere.

  • 05

    Independence

    The child can do it without help.
